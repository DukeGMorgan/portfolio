01

Clinical informatics / AI-assisted schema intelligence

Make a 600+ table clinical database easier to understand.

A schema assistant turns an unfamiliar 600+ table data model into an immediate, governed starting point for investigation and analyst enablement.

The problem

Troubleshooting an enterprise EMR across a multi-clinic network requires knowing how its data fits together. With more than 600 tables, learning the schema becomes part of every investigation and a major bottleneck for analysts.

What I built & enabled

I built an AI-assisted query and schema intelligence tool grounded in the clinical SQL Server data dictionary. Its scope is strictly metadata and schema: it guides investigation structure while enforcing air-tight data boundaries that keep patient records outside the assistant.

What changed

Analysts navigate complex relational schemas in minutes rather than hours, dramatically shortening root-cause investigation cycle times and accelerating onboarding across the clinical systems team.

02

Clinical AI / Enterprise governance & routing architecture

Give clinical AI a controlled place to run.

The data boundary is an architectural mandate that every model, agent, and workflow must respect.

The problem

Deploying AI in healthcare requires knowing which tasks involve protected health information, which environments can receive it under an active BAA, and what scope each agent requires. Useful capabilities need an approved, governed path into production.

What I built & enabled

I authored the enterprise AI business case and established a BAA-governed Azure AI Foundry pathway. I architected a model routing gateway with role-scoped agents and traffic classification: sensitive workloads use BAA-covered or local inference; commercial model work is strictly isolated to metadata, code, and architecture.

What changed

Clinical and operational teams adopt AI with defined execution paths, auditable access boundaries, and zero risk of unauthorized PHI egress.

  2. 02Check its data sensitivity
  3. 03Route to the permitted environment

03 / Supporting engineering

Problems solved in clinical systems.

My experience shows up in the problems I investigate and the tools I build. These examples from my clinical systems work connect the underlying fault to a practical solution.

Clinical record access

Bring four legacy EMRs into one view.

Problem: Records were spread across four legacy platforms, with shared credentials for access.

Solution: I built a read-only clinical viewer and migration tooling with .NET, React, and Azure. The migration covered 3M+ clinical records, 770K+ documents, and 80K+ treatment cycles. Entra ID single sign-on replaced shared credentials, with authorization controls across clinics.

Safer operational changes

Check the system before applying the plan.

Problem: The live system could change between approval and execution.

Solution: My Python/Flask administration console handles chart merges, provisioning, drug libraries, and permissions. It re-validates live state before execution and stops when conditions differ from the approved plan. It caught a real divergence on its first run.

Data integrity

Find the fault behind a successful load.

Problem: A laboratory migration reported success while a non-unique key collapsed distinct records.

Solution: I traced the fault, changed the keying approach, and recovered records that would otherwise have been lost.

EMR troubleshooting

Find charts the system made invisible.

Problem: A vendor function made affected charts impossible to open and invisible in search, making missing records harder to recognize.

Solution: I traced the issue to a null-dereference and produced a reversible fix.

Permission governance

Verify the audit trail behind access changes.

Problem: During access remediation across two production sites, partial permission changes were missing from the vendor’s audit trail.

Solution: I identified the stored-procedure gap, removed excess access, and validated the governed change.

Patient document access

Put access controls around sensitive documents.

Problem: A patient document store allowed public reads, and a separate authorization gap permitted access across clinics.

Solution: I implemented authenticated document delivery, row-level controls, and a global authorization filter.
